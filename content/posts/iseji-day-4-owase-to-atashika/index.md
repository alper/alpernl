![Owase](owase.jpeg)

The train I have to catch this morning only runs after 9am so I thought I'd take the opportunity to get a real breakfast for the first time. [Scale](https://maps.app.goo.gl/TpzjiXTJikwasuqc9) is as good as mentioned and people are lining up before opening time to get in on the action. I attack my plate like a wild animal and am well underway to the FamilyMart and the train station by 08:30.

The train to Mikisato it is. I can read it even without romanization because it's just ‘three’ 三 ‘tree’ 木 ‘village’ 里. That means there's probably two- and four- tree villages out there as well.[^1]

![Train View](train-view.jpeg)

At Mikisato I see a man with a baby and his parents struggling to get on the train. On these single driver operated carriages, there's only one door that opens to regulate the flow of passengers. After pointing out where to get on, I call out: "How did you guys end up here?"
He says he lives there and the train takes off. How do you live somewhere and not know how the trains work?

The goal today is to get to Kumano since I [read that it could be done](https://walkkumano.com/iseji/#day7).

From Mikisato quickly the double whammy of [Miki-toge/Hago-toge](https://www.kodo.pref.mie.lg.jp/en/course/11.html). It rained a lot last night, so everything is slippery. On the trailhead they thoughtfully provide sticks again to ward off the bears.

I'm regretting not bringing my hiking poles *a lot*. I think calling this a ‘walk’ really threw me off. This is very much a hike and a fairly strenuous one at that.

![Stone Path](toge.jpeg)

I don't really see very many shrines today. I'm getting a bit worried until I finally find one.

There is another trail booklet and the people there also report zero bears but say they did see monkeys. I'm sad that I didn't see any monkeys.

Then it's down to Kata. Walking from coastal town to coastal town means going back down to 0 altimeters every time.

![Bridge Construction](kata-bridge.jpeg)

Kata didn't have fishing rights historically. Weird how one town next to the sea would be able to fish and the next wouldn't. It looks like they're getting a new bridge. More infrastructure investment that is impossible to find in Germany.

I sit down and eat some of the snacks I brought. There is no convenience store on this entire stretch which tells you something about how remote it is.

![Statues](statues.jpeg)

The next pass, [Hobo-toge](https://www.kodo.pref.mie.lg.jp/en/course/12.html) goes all the way up to 350m of altitude. About as much as the mountain pass yesterday. It does feel a bunch easier.

After hitting the pass proper, every time I think it's going to go down, another section of climbing follows. So up and down it goes for a while after which I have to drop the entire 300m in very short order to get down to Nigishima.

![Stone Path](stone-path.jpeg)

On the way down it becomes obvious that my knee isn't really having and it takes about as long to get down as it took to get up. With that, the three hours in the description for the pass remain accurate. At that point it is after 2pm so it becomes clear that I'm not going to do another one of these, let alone walk much more and go all the way to Kumano.

So I cancel my plan to push and stick with my already booked accommodation in Atashika. I confirm dinner and breakfast with the host—remember there's absolutely nothing out here—and then wait for the train.

![Nigishima Port](nigishima.jpeg)

There are worse ways to spend two hours than sitting in a gazebo in a tiny port town watching the people drop by to use the public toilet while drinking hot beverages from the vending machine.

Not being high up in the mountains and walking along the train line gives this kind of flexibility. It's possible to change plans and reschedule because there are 4-5 trains still stopping at even this super tiny place.

The train comes at 16:18 as promised and after a short hop to Atashika I walk up to my lodgings. I FaceTime my kids while walking and by the time I get there the sun has set well and proper. You don't really want to be stuck on the backroads after dark here.

![Sea View](sea-view.jpeg)

The guesthouse is run by a lovely septuagenarian retired woman who's studying English and has traveled all over the world. I wondered why people would live in these towns but they moved here after retirement and from up on the hill her living room has a stellar sea view. I think I get it.

![Nabe](nabe.jpeg)

My host cooks me a delicious nabe meal which probably is the best thing I could have eaten.
