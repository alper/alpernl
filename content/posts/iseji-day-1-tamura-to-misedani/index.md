The day starts late after jetlag keeps me up between 2-5am. Then when I finally get to sleep, it's time to get up and leave. The Fitbit clocks me at 3h47m of sleep.

I quickly walk over from the lovely [Mizuhokan](https://maps.app.goo.gl/jPwVTpBdEucQciL5A) to the Ise outer shrine. The site is beautiful and I just learned that the empty lot next to the shrine is where they'll build the building anew every 20 years. A tradition, _shikinen sengu_, I had heard about before but hadn't connected to this place. They started the 63rd rebuilding this year [apparently](https://www.iseshima-kanko.jp/en/highlights/sengu_eg).

Then given permission by [the inspiration for this walk](https://walkkumano.com/iseji/) (the amazing [Craig Mod](https://craigmod.com)) to skip a section, I go to Iseshi station, get some breakfast and wait for the train to Tamaru. Walking [the exact GPS trail](https://walkkumano.com/iseji/gpx/iseji-ise_to_shingu-full_170km_walk.gpx) of somebody else up to and including the road crossings, feels a bit weird, but it's the only way that I can swing doing this in the first place. Having the GPS trails provided is a huge service and more major hikes should do this officially.

I visit the [Higashitokida shrine](https://maps.app.goo.gl/7Yz8W4StCLSnFREC9) quickly and am much impressed by the sense of space the walk in always gives you with the grove of trees and the gravel path.

![Signpost](sign.jpeg)

![Crossing](crossing.jpeg)

It rains almost all day today from the early morning until after noon. Maybe that's why the number of people I see out and about is extremely limited. In the early morning I do see lots of kids cycling to school with their uniforms and helmets on.

I wait out the worst of the rain at [the local post office](https://maps.app.goo.gl/M8bjJ97pLK4wj7TH7) when a man jumps out of a car and asks me where I'm going. Upon hearing Misedani he comments that it's beautiful but the leaves this year haven't turned yet because it's been way too hot.

The colours of the leaves are reported on during the local weather forecasts. Autumn colours in Germany can be very nice as well so why don't they report on them there?

Then after some back and forth between provincial roads, it's time for the [Misesaka Toge](https://www.kodo.pref.mie.lg.jp/en/course/02.html). Not very challenging but a couple of steep segments and switch backs. All in all very beautiful. At one of the guardian deities there is a box with a trail booklet. Thin as it is, it goes back for years because there are so few people hiking this trail. The last person before me who signed the book did so three days ago.

![Stairs of Leaves](stairs.jpeg)

![Shrine](shrine.jpeg)

Then comes a relatively uninspiring part along the main road. I'm hungry and sick of the rain. I cut around the route to make my way to [Cafe de Touhenvoku](https://maps.app.goo.gl/GmwR3n93pkfF595i8) which with its funky interior and pizza toast on the menu reveals itself to be a genuine [Kissa](https://shop.specialprojects.jp/products/kissa-by-kissa-4th-ed?variant=42844316762267). I get the Katsu Curry and scarf it down. How excellent is it to eat something warm after a whole day of walking through the rain?

![Kissaten](kissa-1.jpeg)

![Kissaten](kissa-2.jpeg)

At this point I consider taking the train at Tochihara to skip to the end but feeling my legs still doing well, I loop back to the track and continue along the route. Good that I did, because otherwise I'd have missed the riverbed crossing and the bamboo forest. I'll happily walk a bunch to see amazing stuff like this.

![Standing in a bamboo forest](bamboo.jpeg)

![Riverbed](river.jpeg)

![Iron bridge](bridge.jpeg)

I stop at [bread and coffee](https://maps.app.goo.gl/D8LdNdqDX481cjyP8) in Kawazoe which true to its name sells bread and coffee, and children's toys. I'm not sure what's going on with the population but I'm seeing children everywhere. After that point the walk becomes an incessant plod along the secondary road. My feet are hurting and the light is fading. Sundown at 16:45 does not mean that much if the mountain obscures at least an hour worth of light.

I pass a bunch of derelict buildings and [a very weird one](https://maps.app.goo.gl/Dj9Woz13B7wyxJFP9).

![Mr. Bear on the roadside](kuma.jpeg)

Mr Bear (Kumasan above) did not make it unfortunately.

The walking along the main road is a feature of this walk that will not be appreciated by pure hikers. In the Alps, any route that takes you on more than the absolutely necessary amount of tarmac (noticeable by the sound your walking sticks make) is considered to be unacceptable.

Once I get inside city limits and seeing that it will take another half hour of walking I see a bus is due in 5 minutes. Not giving it a second thought at the [Shimo-Mise bus stop](https://maps.app.goo.gl/zKAc8JarjvusDUbu6) I hop on an entirely empty bus that takes me the rest of the way to the Misedani station. I can barely walk the last bit to the hotel.

That hotel is the [Fairfield](https://maps.app.goo.gl/dttDrTfEW37QCdwU8) which is rather bougie. It also seems to accommodate many foreigners. At least I'm not taking a shower in a communal ofuro today. At Misedani, dinner options during the week are limited with interesting places opening on the weekend for outside pleasure-seekers.

I find a bow of ramen and alcohol free Kirin at a ["famous" ramen place](https://maps.app.goo.gl/VEqWbiDQkmJoi3sc6) where lots of kids sometimse with their parents eat. The ramen is nothing to write home about but the electrolytes and vegetables are welcome. I'm happy too to see that I can still walk.

![Ramen](ramen.jpeg)

The stats for the day say 33,53km and some 42k steps ([strava](https://www.strava.com/activities/16558960888)).

It was a pretty brutal day and I've now established what a reasonable output looks like and that I'll need to round off the walk around 3pm. It's now also a fact that I'm not going to walk to Shingu in five days (a steep goal anyway), so that opens the door to more radical replanning. That would have been necessary anyway for tomorrow where an absurd 40km is on the roster to get to Kihoku.
