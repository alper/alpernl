I still need to write up how I got to Ise in the first place and why that was so terrible.

After landing on Narita at around 11:00 JST, I fumble around a bit and manage to buy myself a sandwich and a [Skyliner](https://www.keisei.co.jp/keisei/tetudou/skyliner/us/skyliner/index.php) ticket. So far so good.

Then getting into town, I quickly find out I can't get any money from the ATMs. Not from any ATM, not with any card.

Then ensues a frantic game of trying things, coming up with scenarios and waiting for Germany to wake up. I have a big stack of "issuer declined" notices from different banks and machines.

What is working is:

- I brought €150 with me which I exchanged for yens.
- I got on WiFi somewhere outside Tokyo station and bought a Shinkansen ticket online with my credit card. This website is pretty broken but thankfully not that broken.

What isn't working:

- Paying for things with my credit card. It seems I changed the PIN code and forgot to put the new one into 1Password.
- We tried exercising our physical credit cards in Berlin and it's not clear to me how or where I would do this. I generally tap my phone at the terminal and that works everywhere. In Japan phone payments only work at the larger shops currently.
- Japan doesn’t really want to give cash out against a credit card is what I learned later.

Now the choice is, stay put or get myself to my accommodation. My next five days of sleeping are already booked, I've just bought my train ticket online, so a large part of the money I exchanged is in reserve but also I’m going to a part of Japan that’s very rural.

I figure that if need be €150 can last me for a week or more if I only eat FamiChicki and other convenience store foods. So I get on the Shinkansen with the QR-code of my digital ticket.

Then at Nagoya station I try some more machines after support at the German bank does some resets. Still nothing is working.

At this point I'm resigned. I buy a train ticket to Iseshi and stare out the window for two hours. When arrived, I get word from Germany that there's been a payment system malfunction in the group of my bank and that lots of people everywhere had issues with their cards.

After six very stressful hours, I've arrived where I should be.

Major lessons learned:

- Bring cash on holiday. If I had brought twice as much, I would not have really stressed out about this.
- Have more than one bank. I really regret closing my Dutch "real" bank account after this.
