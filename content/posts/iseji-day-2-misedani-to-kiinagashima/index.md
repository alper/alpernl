Well before 7am I grab a quick breakfast at the local FamilyMart and supplies for the day ahead. I'm glad that these things are open 24 hours, but how is this possible? Maybe it's better to not ask any questions (s. [Store owner regrets 'slave contracts' with major Japanese convenience store chain Ministop](https://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20251114/p2a/00m/0bu/032000c)). I can't get any kind of coffee at a time like this, but the hotel machine brews something that isn't entirely undrinkable.

{{< figure src="IMG_0067-Large.jpeg" alt="" caption="" >}}

I eat a pudding and go over the plan again. The start will be the first pass just outside of Odai, [Misesaka](https://www.kodo.pref.mie.lg.jp/en/course/02.html) though "just outside" is relative since getting to the trailhead requires a bit of a walk.

{{< figure src="IMG_0072-Large.jpeg" alt="" caption="" >}}

{{< figure src="IMG_0073-Large.jpeg" alt="" caption="" >}}

No rain today for which I'm very thankful which means there are somewhat more people out and about than yesterday. Still the number is very close to zero. Not that many _ohayo_ s this morning.

To my delight and reassurance at the trailhead there is a box where you can borrow a bear bell and grab a walking stick. You then leave it at the other trailhead where there's a similar arrangement. This is extremely considerate and something that's entirely impossible where I'm from.

{{< figure src="IMG_0077-Large.jpeg" alt="" caption="" >}}

{{< figure src="IMG_0084-Large.jpeg" alt="" caption="" >}}

Japan is in a bear frenzy at the moment and there's hardly anything else on the news or on anybody's lips. I can report that the bear bell works, because I didn't see one. Or it could have something to do with how on [Kumamap](https://kumamap.com) this part of Japan is entirely empty.

It's beautiful and a bit of an effort. On the other side, [Takihara shrine](https://en.taiki-okuise.jp/view/takiharaguu/) awaits. This was just before 10am and the monks are all up and about. They're moving stuff around for some kind of event and at one of the buildings a bunch monks are busy with the inner sanctuary.

{{< figure src="IMG_0079-Large.jpeg" alt="" caption="" >}}

{{< figure src="IMG_0085-Large.jpeg" alt="" caption="" >}}

It's cool to see a bunch of men in full regalia doing that stuff even though I don't know (nor probably will ever know) what they are doing. I wonder what the hat with the feather is which turns out to be a [kanmuri with its ei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanmuri_(headwear)#Structure).

As captivating as that is, that doesn't change the fact that either I have to catch the 10:25 at [Takihara station](https://maps.app.goo.gl/iSaBNohHReihSrbP6) or be stuck here during a 2-3 hour hole in the schedule. Double timing through the town is not fun with legs as sore as mine, but I get there with time to spare. The train itself is manned by one person acting as conductor, driver and ticket seller. When he rolls into the station, I see him pointing out the driver's window at something. I try to find the thing he's pointing at until I realize that he's practicing [pointing-and-calling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pointing_and_calling) which is common in Japan.

{{< figure src="IMG_0087-Large.jpeg" alt="" caption="" >}}

{{< figure src="IMG_0095-Large.jpeg" alt="" caption="" >}}

I'd considered taking the train to O-Uchiyama but given how my legs are feeling, any bit of walking that can go should go. I get off at Umegadani and make my way to the trailhead for the [Nisaka](https://www.kodo.pref.mie.lg.jp/en/course/03.html) pass.

{{< figure src="IMG_0098-Large.jpeg" alt="" caption="" >}}

{{< figure src="IMG_0099-Large.jpeg" alt="" caption="" >}}

At Nisaka there's a similar arrangement with the bells and now we also add another dangerous animal to our repertoire: the viper.

{{< figure src="IMG_0101-Large.jpeg" alt="" caption="" >}}

{{< figure src="IMG_0103-Large.jpeg" alt="" caption="" >}}

This is also where the stunning coastal views start. On the way down there's the occasional choice between the Meiji and Edo routes, one meandering and the other straight and vertical.

{{< figure src="IMG_0115-Large.jpeg" alt="" caption="" >}}

{{< figure src="IMG_0114-Large.jpeg" alt="" caption="" >}}

I find myself fighting against the sun on the walk out in… end of November. I'm going to see about picking up some sunscreen in town.

With lunchtime approaching I'm eager to make it into town where I've scoped out some restaurants. Being around at a time when gastronomy is actually open can be a rare and wonderful thing.

I make my way around a small lake where I find [Kappa Club](https://maps.app.goo.gl/8pk2gSR2NpHUAwAT7), a kind of mix between a beach club and a kissaten. There I treat myself to a well-deserved shrimp plate, nama and coffee while I watch several [black kites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_kite) circle over the water. I cannot believe how beautiful this entire arrangement is.

{{< figure src="IMG_0127-Large.jpeg" alt="" caption="" >}}

{{< figure src="IMG_0128-Large.jpeg" alt="" caption="" >}}

A bit further along there's a very promising coffee truck [cafe kiihot](https://maps.app.goo.gl/bspa4M8hZWzHzheCA) which sadly isn't open today.

It's time for me to find my accommodation but not before stopping at the [Cocokarafine](https://maps.app.goo.gl/UdmikCD6wEkVd9HZA) to get some trail care supplies. A big box Japanese drugstore is something you have to see to believe. It's more of a marvel than many of the sights along the Kumano Kodo. The range of products that is on offer in different areas and the sheer assault on the senses is astounding.

{{< figure src="IMG_0130-Large.jpeg" alt="" caption="" >}}

On my way to the guest house, the owner of Kappa finds me on the street and shows me around a repurposed [sento that's now a fishery museum](https://maps.app.goo.gl/jFhGqNXGGytYGsmBA). It's an interesting jumble of stuff he's collected there, but I think the real attraction are their events.

I'm staying at [Toyohisa](https://maps.app.goo.gl/QVg1PBzMZmi6JaWw6) tonight where I have an entire floor to myself. The host wants to make me dinner, after which I'm so full I didn't even think about walking anywhere else for more food. My legs are very grateful. Except for these extremely dangerous stairs I have to go down at night to visit the lavatory.

While writing this I find the video that Craig Mod posted about his day along the route and I can say that's a picture perfect depiction of how my day went as well: [https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0QL5GB2vdS8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0QL5GB2vdS8)

I can't believe this is only day two. More tomorrow.
